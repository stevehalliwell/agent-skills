# Post-StoryBrand checks

Run these after the SB7 BrandScript is coherent. They are practical extensions around StoryBrand, not additional official SB7 elements. Their purpose is to keep a clear customer story from becoming generic, incomplete, manipulative, or poorly fitted to the decision and channel.

## 1. Distinctiveness and voice

- What makes this message recognisably this organisation rather than a competent competitor?
- Does it preserve approved voice, essential terminology, and any decision-critical detail?
- Has clarity editing reduced the message to generic StoryBrand phrasing?

**Result:** retain the evidence-backed differentiator and approved voice; rewrite generic wording without making the message harder to understand.

## 2. Complex buying

Run when a decision involves more than one person or role.

- What shared outcome must the buying group achieve?
- For each evidenced role: what is their concern, perceived risk, proof need, influence, and next action?
- Do role-specific messages support the same shared story, or do their promises and CTAs conflict?

**Result:** create compatible variants from one group-level message. Do not invent stakeholders or treat each role as an unrelated hero.

## 3. Individual or collective success

- Who experiences the problem, who decides, and who benefits?
- Is success individual, relational, organisational, family-level, community-level, or shared across a buying group?
- Does the hero and transformation accurately reflect that level?

**Result:** use a collective protagonist or success statement when that is the real story; do not default to individual transformation.

## 4. Ethical stakes

- Is each villain, failure stake, or urgency claim truthful, proportionate, and dignified?
- Does it avoid invented enemies, shame, stigma, unjustifiable fear, and exploitation of vulnerability?
- Does any risk statement pair with a credible, supported action the customer can take?
- Would a gain-framed alternative communicate the same decision without unnecessary threat?

**Result:** keep credible stakes, revise or remove manipulative framing, and record an evidence or approval gap where needed.

## 5. Enough information for the decision

Run for technical, high-cost, regulated, or consequential decisions.

- What proof, limitations, comparison criteria, implementation detail, requirements, or FAQs does the customer need to decide responsibly?
- Which details belong beside the story rather than inside its concise core?
- Are those details supported and available in the intended channel?

**Result:** attach an evidence layer or supporting asset. Do not strip material decision information merely to make the narrative shorter.

## 6. Asset role in the journey

- What job does this asset have: create curiosity, build understanding, support commitment, onboard, retain, or another explicit job?
- Which BrandScript elements are relevant to that job?
- What is the one appropriate next action?

**Result:** use only the elements the asset needs. Do not force a miniature seven-part story into every ad, page, email, pamphlet, or support message. For pre-purchase campaign staging, use [Curiosity → Enlightenment → Commitment](curiosity-enlightenment-commitment.md).

