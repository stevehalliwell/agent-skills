# Review a brand and its content through StoryBrand

This is a three-part diagnostic, not praise or a generic copy review. It tests whether the message is evident, whether it follows the StoryBrand structure, and whether it conveys the user's intended story.

## Required read

Read [../references/storybrand-framework.md](../references/storybrand-framework.md). Done when the seven-part structure and claim limits are known.

## Part 1: inspect the existing brand and material

1. Ask the user to point to the existing brand and the material to review: website URL or local path, pamphlet, proposal, campaign, page, or other body of work. Establish whether its scope is a brand, division, offer, campaign, or other decision context; gather the existing BrandScript and its source where available, audience, channel, desired action, constraints, requested review depth, approved claims, and evidence. Record local-source path, origin or permission note, claim boundary, and preservation boundary where applicable. Done when the review corpus, evidence boundary, script scope, and requested review scope are known.
2. Read or inspect the supplied material and extract the StoryBrand it actually communicates. Record an evidence-backed provisional answer for every SB7 element and sub-point; mark anything not communicated as `Missing`, and do not fill gaps with assumptions. State any inaccessible or out-of-scope material. Done when the review corpus is available and its communicated StoryBrand is mapped, or the blocker is explicit.

## Part 2: establish the user's intended StoryBrand independently

3. Ask the user the full SB7 question set independently of the material's apparent answers. Do not skip a question because the material seems to answer it, and do not frame a question with the provisional interpretation from Part 1. Ask dependent questions one at a time.
   - **Hero:** Who is the single customer or client hero? What specific, tangible quest are they pursuing? Does this scope need a separate script from another audience, offer, or desired outcome?
   - **Problem:** What root cause or villain focuses the issue, if any? What external problem blocks that quest? How does it make the hero feel? Why should that situation not be acceptable?
   - **Guide:** How should the organisation help the hero's journey? What empathy is credible? What evidence supports its authority?
   - **Plan:** What is the short process plan: first action, next steps, and supported outcome? What approved agreement, policy, standard, or guarantee would reduce a relevant fear? Is a paradigm shift needed to help the customer understand the problem?
   - **CTA:** What direct action should they take now? What genuine lower-commitment action is available?
   - **Failure stakes:** What credible practical or emotional cost follows from doing nothing?
   - **Success outcome:** What tangible change does the hero achieve, how does it enable their goal, and what supported transformation takes them from their current state to a better one?
4. Record the user's answers as the intended StoryBrand, separately from the material's communicated StoryBrand. Distinguish confirmed intent and evidence from `Assumption` and `Question`. Done when both StoryBrand versions are mapped well enough to compare, or remaining gaps are explicit.

## Part 3: deliver the critical review

5. Review the material on all three axes:
   - **Message evident:** On a quick read, can the intended audience identify themselves as the hero, their quest, their problem, the guide, the action to take, and why it matters? Cite the content location that proves or fails each point.
   - **StoryBrand alignment:** Critically interrogate every SB7 section and sub-point from the framework: character/hero (recognition and quest); problem (optional villain/root cause, external, internal, philosophical, and their connection); guide (journey role, empathy, authority); plan (process, optional agreement, optional paradigm); CTA (direct and transitional); failure stakes; and success outcome (including optional character transformation). Mark each `Good`, `Partial`, `Missing`, or `Unsupported`.
   - **Intent alignment:** Compare the material's communicated StoryBrand with the user's intended StoryBrand. Identify every mismatch, omission, distortion, contradiction, unsupported promise, or competing priority.
6. Diagnose the source of each issue. State whether it is: **a poor StoryBrand** (the intended story itself is weak, incomplete, contradictory, or unsupported); **poor communication** (the intended story is sound but the material fails to convey it); or **a mismatch** (the material conveys a different story from the user's intent). Assess each story as a connected whole: whether the recognisable hero pursues a tangible quest; the problem credibly obstructs it; the organisation remains the guide; the plan gives the hero a believable path; the CTA asks for the appropriate next step; and failure and success make that choice meaningful. Identify all broken links, contradictions, competing heroes, unsupported leaps, and points where the story loses momentum. Done when the review states whether the issue is in the story, its communication, or both, and whether the material tells one compelling, coherent customer story.
7. Read [../references/post-storybrand-checks.md](../references/post-storybrand-checks.md) and run every check, marking genuinely inapplicable checks as such. Diagnose whether each failure is in the underlying story, its communication, or the wider decision/channel context. Done when every post-StoryBrand check has a finding and remedy.
8. Be critical and direct. A sound element may simply be marked `Good`; do not add praise padding or soften criticism. Every element that is weak, missing, wrong, unsupported, or misaligned—and every coherence failure—must have a remedy: replacement wording when evidence permits, otherwise the different approach, decision, or evidence needed. When requested, add content direction, a rewrite, newly generated channel copy, or a revised BrandScript. Ground every addition in supplied evidence; label unsupported additions `Assumption` or `Question`. Done when all three axes and the post-StoryBrand checks have findings, every criticism has a remedy, and any requested direction, draft, or revision is grounded in the supplied material and evidence.
