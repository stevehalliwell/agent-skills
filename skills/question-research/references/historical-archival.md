# Historical and Archival Research

Use this reference for origin, attribution, terminology, historical narrative, or primary-text inquiries.

## Coverage lenses

- **Primary record:** contemporaneous publications, drafts, correspondence, proceedings, records, or direct testimony appropriate to the claim.
- **Transmission:** citations, reprints, translations, later attribution, and the distinction between an origin claim and a claim that someone repeated or popularized it.
- **Language and names:** original-language terms, historical spellings, translations, transliterations, and name variants.
- **Context:** enough surrounding text and date context to distinguish literal wording, rhetoric, paraphrase, and later interpretation.
- **Access:** digitized, physically accessible, catalogued but unavailable, or unknown. Treat access status as evidence limit, not absence of the record.
- **Alternatives:** competing attributions, explanations, and chronology.

## Discovery and capture

Start with primary-source candidates and cited-reference chains. Search relevant library catalogues, archives, proceedings, institutional collections, digitized books, and historical-language variants alongside ordinary web results.

Retain the quoted passage plus page, section, document date, edition, archive identifier, and translation provenance where applicable. A secondary account can locate a primary source, but does not establish a stronger attribution than its own evidence permits.

## Synthesis

Distinguish what the accessible record establishes from what it cannot. End with an archival follow-up list: named records, repositories or likely custodians, and the specific uncertainty each record could resolve.
