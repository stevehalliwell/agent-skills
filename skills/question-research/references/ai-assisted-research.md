# AI-Assisted Research Workflow

Use this reference for every inquiry that asks an AI to discover, interpret, or summarize sources.

Source: ["How good is AI at historical research?"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qEd9DTMXAOk), *Waiting Through AI*, retrieved 2026-09-15. The discussion is workflow guidance, not evidence about a research subject.

## Working rules

- Treat AI output as orientation, source discovery, and a prompt for further checking—not as proof or a complete account.
- Preserve the discovery path: searches, source trails, rejected leads, and why the next action was taken.
- State what a source says, not a stronger conclusion. A secondary report, blog post, or personal account remains that source's report unless corroborated by appropriate primary evidence.
- For every material claim, retain the exact passage or bounded extract and a stable source location. Label direct observation, paraphrase, inference, hypothesis, and unresolved claim separately.
- `Unknown`, inaccessible, non-digitized, and ambiguous are valid results. Never supply a plausible explanation merely to complete a narrative.
- Separate source lists and exploratory leads from synthesis. When framing could bias a human's interpretation, offer source-discovery mode before an AI-written overview.
- After a first pass, identify missing sources, alternative explanations, and records that would resolve open questions. Do not imply the inquiry is exhaustive.

## Review questions

Before accepting a material claim, ask:

1. Does the cited passage support this exact wording?
2. Is the report confusing a source's assertion with an established fact?
3. What primary record, original-language text, or inaccessible archive could change the answer?
4. What source, alternative explanation, or unusual detail might the overview have omitted?
