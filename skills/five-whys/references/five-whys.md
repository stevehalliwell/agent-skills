# Five Whys method

Five Whys is structured inquiry into causal conditions or intended purpose. Its value is not reaching exactly five questions; it is exposing assumptions, evidence gaps, and useful next actions.

## Use

Start from one observed outcome, decision, behavior, or proposed purpose. Ask an open question about condition, mechanism, motivation, or intent. Map each response, then choose next question that most reduces uncertainty.

Cause diagnosis examples: a deployment failed, players abandon a level, a feature is unused, a process is slow. Purpose discovery examples: why a product capability exists, why a scene needs an event, why a design constraint matters.

## Evidence discipline

Separate observation, reported experience, inference, and hypothesis. A causal chain may branch; multiple contributing conditions can coexist. Look for disconfirming evidence and distinguish proximate cause from contributing condition or systemic context.

## Limits and safe stopping

Stop when an action is clear, new answers only repeat prior claims, evidence is unavailable, or user does not want to continue. Do not force five questions. Do not use method to assign individual blame, justify a predetermined conclusion, or claim proof from a plausible story.

Next step may be gather evidence, run an experiment, choose an intervention, or leave a named open question.
