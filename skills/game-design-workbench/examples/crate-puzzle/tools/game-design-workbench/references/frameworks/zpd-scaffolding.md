# Supported challenge and scaffolding

Pack: `zpd-scaffolding` · version 1
Attribution: Lev Vygotsky: zone of proximal development; David Wood, Jerome Bruner, Gail Ross: scaffolding/tutoring (1976)

## Scope

Explore temporary support, independent performance, demonstrations, practice, and withdrawal of help in a selected learning path.

Use only when this lens is selected. Follow [review operation](../review.md); do not repair source during review.

## Inputs and query support

Roles: `skills`, `challenges`, `levels`, `observations`. Read actual role mapping and live schema/usage before writing.

Available recipes: `query skills`; `query routes`; `query records --collection <mapped-alias>`. These are declared-data projections, not automated framework verdicts. Inspect linked bodies for conditions and evidence.

## Original prompts

1. What could the learner attempt independently, and what currently needs support? Which part is only an assumption?
2. Could a hint reveal a relevant feature without choosing the whole solution?
3. Where is safe practice available before a consequence becomes costly?
4. Does a worked example expose the reasoning or only a sequence to copy?
5. How might support become less specific as understanding develops?
6. What later, changed task would let the designer observe independent transfer?

## Missing data and evidence limits

Missing support is a question, not automatic failure. Proceed without level records; inspect challenge entry/next links and explicit role lists.

ZPD and later scaffolding are related but not identical. No teaching prerequisite, developmental diagnosis, or proof that a player cannot succeed without a declared introduction. Routes are declared structural opportunities, not gameplay reachability.

## Fixture application

Push-room can lead directly to combined-room, bypassing switch-practice. Ask whether support is intentional on that route; do not assert impossible completion.
