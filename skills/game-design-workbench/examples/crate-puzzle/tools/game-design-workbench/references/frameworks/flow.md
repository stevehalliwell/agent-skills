# Flow: challenge and capability

Pack: `flow` · version 1
Attribution: Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience (1990)

## Scope

Explore challenge relative to assumed capability, clear goals, feedback, and conditions for sustained attention in a selected encounter.

Use only when this lens is selected. Follow [review operation](../review.md); do not repair source during review.

## Inputs and query support

Roles: `skills`, `challenges`, `mechanics`, `hypotheses`, `observations`. Read actual role mapping and live schema/usage before writing.

Available recipes: `query skills`; `query routes`; `query evidence`. These are declared-data projections, not automated framework verdicts. Inspect linked bodies for conditions and evidence.

## Original prompts

1. What makes the immediate goal understandable before the player commits to an action?
2. What feedback lets the player judge progress while there is still time to adapt?
3. Which assumed capability makes this demand manageable, and what supports that assumption?
4. Where could challenge rise abruptly without a corresponding chance to develop familiarity?
5. What optional assistance or self-selected challenge could accommodate different current capabilities?
6. What would a playtest need to observe before calling this absorption rather than confusion or mere persistence?

## Missing data and evidence limits

State capability assumptions explicitly. Missing observations leave experience unverified; they do not justify predicting anxiety.

No required cohort/profile dataset, calibrated capability curve, flow score, anxiety score, or boredom score. Keep separate from GameFlow. Authored challenge/rank numbers do not measure a player state.

## Fixture application

Examine optional bypass from push-room to combined-room. Ask about feedback and familiarity; do not infer a capability curve from skill ranks.
