# Emotional experience — reference scope and sources

Shared support for the existing frameworks, **not a nineteenth framework**. Use [working vocabulary](emotions.md) for terms, targets, possible elicitors, and distinctions; use [framing procedure](../../workflows/emotional-experience.md) for a particular brief or emotional sequence. Both are provisional, independently authored workbench guidance.

Scope spans simple direct games and intricate emotional intentions. No compulsory taxonomy interview, audience stereotype, prescribed emotional arc, fixed word list, or dictionary-recognition gate. Source provenance tells readers which meaning is in use; it does not decide whether a supplied experience is legitimate.

## Meaning and evidence contract

Definitions, targets, and illustrative eliciting situations in the vocabulary are **original working interpretations**, not verbatim published definitions, validated constructs, or proven emotion-generation rules. A row's source supports a particular meaning or example; it does not establish all uses, universal boundaries, or guaranteed game effects.

Keep these separate:

- Experiencer; target of a feeling; perceived event/appraisal that might elicit it; bibliographic source for a word or account.
- A source's theoretical definition; an empirical association in a particular study; an original design inference; a user's supplied sense.
- Emotion episode, mood, relationship, expression, motive, perceived capability, and focused state. Boundaries can vary with source and intended use; do not police the user's vocabulary.

Table groups are navigation only. Entries marked EDITORIAL deliberately expose an ordinary-language convention or extension where the inspected evidence does not establish that full wording. USER records supplied meaning. Neither label claims empirical validation; neither makes the word unusable. A limited source set is not an excuse to present invented definitions as scholarly findings.

No source establishes that a named mechanic guarantees a feeling, that all cultures express it identically, that repeated failure is necessary for triumph, or that a particular dopamine mechanism explains the designed experience. No prevalence, therapeutic, diagnostic, or emotional-recognition claim is adopted here.

## Source keys

### DESMET

Pieter M. A. Desmet (2012), *Faces of Product Pleasure: 25 Positive Emotions in Human-Product Interactions*, **International Journal of Design 6(2), 1–29**, published August 31, 2012.

- [Publisher article](https://ijdesign.org/index.php/IJDesign/article/view/1190/459).
- [Full local reading conversion](sources/desmet-2012/article.md), [unchanged HTML](sources/desmet-2012/article.html), [retained Figure 1](sources/desmet-2012/Figure1.png), [capture method/hashes](sources/desmet-2012/capture.json).
- Inspected introduction/typology, Stage 2 clustering/appraisal discussion, Six Sources of Emotions, General Discussion, and **all Figure 1 definitions**. The first-pass analyst did not inspect the figure; parent inspected it later. Do not use that first-pass limitation as a current claim that these definitions are unavailable.
- Figure 1 gives concise meanings for the positive terms used here, including sympathy, kindness, respect, love, admiration, dreaminess, lust, desire, worship, euphoria, joy, amusement, hope, anticipation, surprise, energized, courage, pride, confidence, inspiration, enchantment, fascination, relief, relaxation, and satisfaction. Vocabulary wording and arrangement remain original; it is not a reproduction of the typology.
- Stage 2 distinguishes similar appraisals/tendencies and explains why some words are clustered. Six Sources distinguishes object, meaning, interaction, activity, self, and others. Meaning-focus and Activity-focus explicitly show why the visible product need not be the emotional target. General Discussion supplies contentment/savoring and acknowledges mood/trait senses.
- Authority: primary for the author's typology-building and retrospective product-experience study; explanatory synthesis for psychological claims it cites. Recall, prompts, recruitment, product context, and author choices limit transfer. Do not turn its source grouping into mandatory categories or a proven model of all negative/game emotions. Other tables/figures are not visually audited.

### LAZZARO

Nicole Lazzaro / XEODesign (2004), *Why We Play Games: Four Keys to More Emotion in Player Experiences*.

- [Existing full local report](../four-keys/captures/why-we-play-games/why_we_play_games.md); [provenance and limits](../four-keys/README.md). No new acquisition.
- Inspected Emotions During Play and tables/discussion in §§1a, 2a–2c, 3a/3c, 4a/4d. These situate fiero, naches/kvell, schadenfreude, wonder, awe, amusement, curiosity, frustration, relief, excitement, and related experiences. The report's awe usage includes dread; vocabulary's broader awe sense is explicitly EDITORIAL.
- Authority: primary practitioner field report for its observations and usage; secondary for psychological literature it cites. Its qualitative sample, reporting tensions, mixed practitioner/participant accounts, and conversion defects remain recorded in the Four Keys guide. No universal gesture detector, neuroscience conclusion, or required frustration recipe follows.
- Naches concerns child/mentee accomplishment in this source. Kvell concerns expressing that pride to others. The report does not establish a dictionary history or every language/community's usage.

### OCC-REVIEW

Bas R. Steunebrink, Mehdi Dastani, and John-Jules Ch. Meyer, *The OCC Model Revisited*, author-hosted eight-page paper, commonly cited as 2009; venue metadata not verified from the extracted copy.

- [Author-hosted PDF](https://people.idsia.ch/~steunebrink/Publications/KI09_OCC_revisited.pdf).
- Inspected §2 and **Table 1, PDF page 3**, which reports the original account's specifications; §3 discusses consequence/event wording, prospects, defaults, and temporal relations. Terms used here include joy/distress, hopes/fears, satisfaction/disappointment/relief, self/other action appraisal, others' fortunes, gratification/remorse/gratitude/anger.
- Authority: expert-secondary/computational reading of Ortony, Clore, and Collins (1988), not direct inspection of that book; primary for the review authors' proposed formal clarification. They explicitly identify their computer-science/logical perspective. Neither its hierarchy nor its intensity variables are adopted by this skill.
- Keep Table 1 distinct from **Table 2's proposed revision**. In particular, do not adopt unfamiliarity as a necessary definition of disgust, or infer that every ordinary meaning follows its formal structure. Prospective events include uncertain past/current events, not only the future. Its narrow resentment/shame specifications are not complete everyday definitions.
- Regret receives a brief lost-opportunity example, not the full general definition used in the vocabulary; the latter remains EDITORIAL. Fear tokens include anxious, so this source does not establish the vocabulary's working anxiety/fear distinction. No full PDF retained; public access was not treated as a republication licence.

### SMITH-ELLSWORTH

Craig A. Smith and Phoebe C. Ellsworth (1985), *Patterns of Cognitive Appraisal in Emotion*, **Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 48(4), 813–838**.

- [Inspected article copy](https://emotionresearcher.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/SE1985.pdf).
- Relevant locators: Method/Subjects and Stimulus Emotions, p. 820; Properties of the Emotions, pp. 831–834; Future Directions, pp. 835–837. Directly checked pp. 832–834 for happiness/pride, interest/hope, surprise, boredom, shame/guilt, disgust, anger/contempt, frustration, sadness, and fear. No full PDF retained.
- Authority: primary study of recalled experiences under supplied labels, with selected Stanford undergraduates. Recalled appraisal profiles are associations, not a demonstrated emotion-causing algorithm or population-wide lexical definition. Broader vocabulary meanings remain working adaptations.
- Shame/guilt and anger/contempt were not cleanly differentiated in this study. Do not turn a convenient working contrast into a universal disjoint boundary. Its pleasant surprise examples do not mean surprise is always pleasant; its low-control sadness accounts do not make helplessness necessary for every sadness.

### NOBA

Brett Ford and Iris B. Mauss, *Emotion Experience and Well-Being*, Noba teaching module; original publication date not established from the current page.

- [Module](https://nobaproject.com/modules/emotion-experience-and-well-being).
- Inspected sections on context, co-occurring emotions, expression/experience coherence, and Vocabulary. The text discusses nostalgia as an affectionate sadness about the past, embarrassment after a social faux pas, mixed pleasant/unpleasant feelings, and why observable responses need not co-occur in every person/instance.
- Authority: expert educational synthesis, not a game study. It supports context/coexistence cautions and the nostalgia example, not every editorial definition, clinical inference, or a rule that unpleasant emotion is beneficial. Its cultural examples are source-reported uses, not universal translations.
- Page states **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. No full chapter, photos, or rating materials retained. Two other Noba modules were fetched as leads (Positive Psychology, Social Anxiety) but are not definition authorities for this vocabulary. Social Anxiety's clinical scope is not imported into ordinary design intentions.

### EDITORIAL

Independently authored working conventions in this reference. Examples include broad regret, anxiety, dread, suspense, despair, confusion, aesthetic pleasure, yearning, loneliness, envy/jealousy distinction, broader resentment, embarrassment, contempt, grief, calm, felt belonging, and being moved/mixed feeling. Several words are named or illustrated in inspected literature, but those appearances do not establish the complete definition or target/elicitor wording here.

These entries are useful design language, not source-validated lexical/clinical definitions. Ask what the designer means when a different sense matters; source-check a specific disputed published meaning if needed. Do not manufacture an etymology, source quotation, instrument score, or cultural claim to fill a gap.

### USER

Meaning supplied in the project conversation: **Scheißenbedauer** for immediate regret after sending a message. This is a supplied usage, not an origin claim, proof of dictionary status, or a definition requiring external endorsement. The user's point is broad expressive range, not investigation of this particular word. Other supplied or coined meanings are equally admissible when their intended sense is clear.

## Local files and rights

`emotions.md` and the framing procedure are original guidance; their definitions and groupings are not an official source instrument. Research artifacts retain provenance, bounded quotations, provisional suggestions, and rejected leads. They do not replace primary sources.

Desmet's article-specific notice states **CC BY-NC-ND 2.5**, copyright © 2012 Pieter Desmet, with first publication rights granted to International Journal of Design. Captured site footer instead states **CC BY 4.0**. This discrepancy is unresolved; retain the conservative local-only disposition rather than claim the footer clears this article or conversion. Original HTML and Figure 1 are retained unchanged with source URLs/hashes. Markdown is a local reading conversion with localized Figure 1 link, not an unchanged published copy. Other figure/table links remain remote; unretained figures were not silently inferred. The conversion is **local-only/not-publication-cleared**; non-commercial/no-derivatives restrictions need review before redistribution. No licence from this source is asserted over unrelated workbench files.

Other new full-text copyrighted papers, instruments, dictionaries, and response sheets are not copied into the package. Existing Lazzaro material retains its own rights limits. Public access, a source citation, user provision, or a local checkpoint does not establish publication rights. Current final branch review gate still applies before push/completion; no commit or publication performed for this expansion.
