# Cognitive load and unfamiliar coordination

Pack: `cognitive-load` · version 1
Attribution: John Sweller, Cognitive Load During Problem Solving: Effects on Learning (1988)

## Scope

Examine unfamiliar operations, concurrent coordination, problem-solving effort, and information presentation in a selected challenge.

Use only when this lens is selected. Follow [review operation](../review.md); do not repair source during review.

## Inputs and query support

Roles: `skills`, `challenges`, `mechanics`, `observations`. Read actual role mapping and live schema/usage before writing.

Available recipes: `query skills`; `query routes`; `query records --collection <mapped-alias>`. These are declared-data projections, not automated framework verdicts. Inspect linked bodies for conditions and evidence.

## Original prompts

1. What must be coordinated at the same time, rather than merely appearing somewhere in the challenge?
2. Which parts are assumed familiar, and what source or observation supports that assumption?
3. Could searching for the next move consume attention needed to understand the underlying rule?
4. What information is unnecessarily hidden, split across views, or repeatedly reinterpreted?
5. Would a demonstration, reduced task, or stable cue expose the relationship without removing the intended decision?
6. What observation would tell us whether assistance supports learning rather than only helping complete this instance?

## Missing data and evidence limits

Ask about concurrent demand and familiarity when absent. Preserve them as assumptions rather than inventing player capability data.

A required-skills list does not establish simultaneous demand. No count-based cognitive-load score or diagnosis. Game-design application is a hypothesis; original laboratory findings are not calibrated mechanic-count limits.

## Fixture application

Coordinate has two prerequisite chains. Inspect challenge prose before claiming both must be actively maintained at once.
