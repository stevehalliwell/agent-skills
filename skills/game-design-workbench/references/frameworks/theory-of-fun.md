# Learning, patterns, and fun

Pack: `theory-of-fun` · version 1
Attribution: Raph Koster, A Theory of Fun for Game Design; 2003 presentation and book

## Scope

Ask what understanding a selected progression invites and whether repetition serves its intended experience.

Use only when this lens is selected. Follow [review operation](../review.md); do not repair source during review.

## Inputs and query support

Roles: `skills`, `challenges`, `mechanics`, `hypotheses`, `observations`. Read actual role mapping and live schema/usage before writing.

Available recipes: `query skills`; `query routes`; `query evidence`. These are declared-data projections, not automated framework verdicts. Inspect linked bodies for conditions and evidence.

## Original prompts

1. What new pattern could the player discover here rather than merely execute faster?
2. Does the next challenge combine familiar ideas in a new way or only change a number?
3. When repetition is intended, what experience does it support besides novelty?
4. Which once-interesting decision becomes automatic, and what replaces it?
5. Could an expert find a new interpretation while a beginner still learns the basic pattern?
6. What observed choices would distinguish discovery, fluent enjoyment, and disengaged repetition?

## Missing data and evidence limits

Use authored skill/challenge descriptions when progression links are incomplete. Lack of observed novelty is not evidence of boredom.

No fun score. Learning is a perspective, not an exhaustive explanation of enjoyment. Repeated actions can support mastery, comfort, expression, or other intentions; do not label repetition defective without context.

## Fixture application

Combined-room combines push planning and switch routing; ask what new relationship the combination teaches instead of assuming harder means deeper.
