# GameFlow enjoyment conditions

Pack: `gameflow` · version 1
Attribution: Penelope Sweetser and Peta Wyeth (2005)

## Scope

Use applicable enjoyment conditions to question a selected mechanic, encounter, or session: concentration, challenge, skills, control, goals, feedback, immersion, and social interaction.

Use only when this lens is selected. Follow [review operation](../review.md); do not repair source during review.

## Inputs and query support

Roles: `mechanics`, `challenges`, `controls`, `hypotheses`, `observations`. Read actual role mapping and live schema/usage before writing.

Available recipes: `query routes`; `query controls`; `query evidence`; `query records --collection <mapped-alias>`. These are declared-data projections, not automated framework verdicts. Inspect linked bodies for conditions and evidence.

## Original prompts

1. What deserves attention now, and what competing presentation could obscure it?
2. Do controls let the player express the intended decision without unexplained loss of control?
3. How are immediate goals and longer-term progress made legible?
4. What feedback distinguishes a misunderstood rule from an unsuccessful strategy?
5. Which elements support sustained involvement, and which interruptions are intentional?
6. If social interaction matters here, what activity enables it? If it does not, why is that criterion inapplicable?

## Missing data and evidence limits

Mark social criteria inapplicable for an intentionally solo experience. Missing goal/feedback prose is a documentation question, not proof players cannot understand the game.

These are original questions, not a reproduction or scoring implementation of the published evaluation instrument. No aggregate enjoyment score. Flow theory and GameFlow are distinct. Control records are adjustable parameters, not a complete account of player agency or input controls.

## Fixture application

Check blocked-push feedback and visible switch state. Social interaction is outside this solo puzzle scope, not a missing mandatory feature.
