# Forms and freedoms of play

Pack: `caillois` · version 1
Attribution: Roger Caillois, Man, Play and Games (1958; English translation by Meyer Barash)

## Scope

Explore competition, chance, make-believe, vertigo, and movement between free improvisation and rule-bound challenge.

Use only when this lens is selected. Follow [review operation](../review.md); do not repair source during review.

## Inputs and query support

Roles: `mechanics`, `dynamics`, `challenges`, `aesthetics`. Read actual role mapping and live schema/usage before writing.

Available recipes: `query mda`; `query routes`; `query records --collection <mapped-alias>`. These are declared-data projections, not automated framework verdicts. Inspect linked bodies for conditions and evidence.

Vocabulary: Agon (competition), alea (chance), mimicry (make-believe), ilinx (vertigo); paidia/ludus describe contrasting tendencies toward improvisation and disciplined rule-bound play.

## Original prompts

1. What is uncertain because of another participant, chance, hidden information, or player interpretation?
2. What freedom to improvise remains inside the current rules?
3. Does role-play or make-believe change the available experience, or is it only surface description?
4. If sensory disorientation matters, what experience and accessibility boundaries are intended?
5. Which tension between free play and prescribed challenge is purposeful?
6. Would adding another form of play support the current goal or merely satisfy an unnecessary category checklist?

## Missing data and evidence limits

Absent forms can be deliberate boundaries. Ask for rules/presentation context rather than inferring chance or sensory experience from theme.

Historical conceptual lens, not a numeric design profile or universal taxonomy test. No requirement to include competition, randomness, role-play, or vertigo.

## Fixture application

Deterministic puzzle rules emphasize prescribed challenge; hypothetical player delay is an experiment assumption, not proof the game itself uses chance.
