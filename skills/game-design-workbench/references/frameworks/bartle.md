## Scope

Use historically situated player/world and acting/interacting distinctions to question activities and tensions in multiplayer designs.

Use only when this lens is selected. Follow [review operation](../review.md); do not repair source during review.

## Inputs and query support

Roles: `mechanics`, `dynamics`, `challenges`, `decisions`, `observations`. Read actual role mapping and live schema/usage before writing.

Available recipes: `query mda`; `query decisions`; `query evidence`; `query records --collection <mapped-alias>`. These are declared-data projections, not automated framework verdicts. Inspect linked bodies for conditions and evidence.

## Working concepts

Vocabulary: Achievers, Explorers, Socialisers, Killers describe approaches discussed in multiplayer MUD research; they are not universal identities.

- **Historical scope:** The article concerns multiplayer MUD participation and relationships among activities. Apply that context before borrowing the familiar labels for another game.
- **World and participants:** Distinguish activities directed toward the game world from those directed toward other people. A non-player character is not automatically equivalent to another participant in this frame.
- **Acting and interacting:** Examine whether an activity emphasizes exerting effects or engaging with and understanding its target. Use the distinction to discuss concrete activity, not to diagnose a person.
- **Approaches and tensions:** Achieving, exploring, socialising, and exerting influence over others can coexist or change over time. One encouraged activity can also undermine another participant’s experience; inspect the actual rules and boundaries.

## Apply to selected slice

1. Check whether the selected question involves meaningful multiplayer participation. If it does not, report limited applicability and stop this lens. Do not invent social systems to make the frame fit.
2. For applicable scope, name actual participant activities and their targets. Read rules, dynamic hypotheses, challenge descriptions, and relevant decisions rather than assigning labels from genre.
3. Use the two distinctions to compare approaches to those activities. Allow the same person to pursue different approaches at different times. Mark uncertain mappings as interpretations.
4. Inspect interactions between approaches, including consent, disruption, recovery, and shared incentives where relevant. Distinguish intended competitive effects from assumptions about harmful behavior.
5. Return the activity tension and its design context. No player-type percentages, population balancing prescription, psychological labels, or requirement to support all four approaches is justified.

## Original prompts

1. Which activities concern changing the world, understanding it, interacting with people, or exerting influence over them?
2. What makes these activities depend on other participants rather than only non-player systems?
3. Could one encouraged activity prevent another participant from enjoying a different activity?
4. What consent, recovery, or boundary rule contains harmful conflict where relevant?
5. Can the same person pursue different approaches at different times?
6. Is this historical multiplayer frame useful for the selected game, or would applying it distort the design?

## Worked example

- **Authored facts:** The crate puzzle is intentionally solo and supplies no multiplayer participation rules. A switch or crate is not another human participant.
- **Applicability result:** The historical MUD frame has limited use for the selected puzzle. Report that boundary rather than mapping every puzzle activity into a player type.
- **Rejected inference:** Solving a puzzle does not make its player an Achiever in a validated universal taxonomy. Likewise, moving a crate forcefully does not make the activity equivalent to acting on other players.
- **Useful next question:** If a later task explicitly introduces multiplayer participation, what participant activities and tensions need inspection? That would be a new supplied scope, not a repair required by this review.

## Evidence and counterchecks

- For an applicable multiplayer design, observe activities and their effects on other participants. Labels alone do not explain why a conflict or cooperation pattern occurs.
- Separate a temporary approach from a stable identity. A person exploring now may compete or socialise later under different conditions.
- Historical observations do not establish category percentages or optimal population ratios for a different design. Any such local claim needs separate evidence.

## Common traps

- Do not apply the taxonomy universally after removing its multiplayer context.
- Do not equate Killers with all combat, all competition, or a psychological diagnosis.
- Stopping an inapplicable lens is a valid outcome; it does not require recommending multiplayer features.

## Missing data and evidence limits

If multiplayer participation is absent, report limited applicability and stop this lens instead of inventing social features.

No universal player typing, percentage allocation, psychological diagnosis, or requirement to serve every category. Do not translate Killers directly into all combat or competitive play without context.
