# Four Keys to Fun

Pack: `four-keys` · version 1
Attribution: Nicole Lazzaro / XEODesign; Why We Play Games research (2004)

## Scope

Explore possible routes to emotion through challenge/triumph, curiosity, meaningful engagement, and social amusement.

Use only when this lens is selected. Follow [review operation](../review.md); do not repair source during review.

## Inputs and query support

Roles: `dynamics`, `aesthetics`, `interactions`, `observations`. Read actual role mapping and live schema/usage before writing.

Available recipes: `query mda`; `query matrix`; `query evidence`; `query records --collection <mapped-alias>`. These are declared-data projections, not automated framework verdicts. Inspect linked bodies for conditions and evidence.

Vocabulary: Hard Fun, Easy Fun, Serious Fun, People Fun are Lazzaro’s labels. They are not synonyms for MDA aesthetics or Koster’s learning account.

## Original prompts

1. What sequence could make overcoming this obstacle feel like earned triumph?
2. What invites curiosity before the player knows the solution?
3. What could make the activity personally meaningful or emotionally restorative in this intended context?
4. If people play together, what shared activity could create amusement rather than merely co-presence?
5. How could the emotional tone change within a session without losing the main intention?
6. Which described player reaction would support or contradict the proposed emotional route?

## Missing data and evidence limits

Keep undeclared social experience inapplicable when appropriate. Do not treat missing observed emotion as evidence of absent emotion.

No category quota, emotional score, copied questionnaire, or rule that every game needs every key. Links to emotional categories remain interpretations until supported by relevant evidence.

## Fixture application

A successful combined-room solution may invite triumph; discovering a switch relation may invite curiosity. Both are hypotheses, not observed reactions.
