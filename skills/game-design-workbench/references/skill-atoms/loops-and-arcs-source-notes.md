## Author's distinctions

These notes report Cook's analysis. They do not establish universal learning or market effects.

### Repetition serves model revision

Cook connects a player's expectations, intervention, system response, and interpretation in a repeating process. Loops can operate at different scales and can depend on one another. His emphasis is not repetition by itself: a useful mastery loop provides causally informative feedback and opportunities to test understanding. Location: **Loops**.

This adds a useful qualification to the earlier [Chemistry essay](chemistry-of-game-design-source-notes.md): a chain should describe what relationships become understandable, not merely enumerate repeated gestures.

### Arcs deliver information without requiring another pass

Cook contrasts a loop with an interaction whose meaningful payload can be consumed and then left behind. Reading or viewing can change a mental model without prompting a return to that same interaction. His compact source definition is: “An arc is a broken loop you exit immediately.” Location: **Arcs**.

He associates arcs with conveying selected accounts of experience, while repeated experimentation can expose a broader set of system behaviors. These are tendencies in his model, not proof that reading cannot build robust understanding or that replaying cannot reveal new meaning.

### A recorded path is not the entire system

Cook distinguishes a description of a single passage through a loop from the range of outcomes that the loop permits. Unrolling a playthrough into sequential events can document that playthrough while hiding alternatives. Location: **Arcs**, discussion of an expanded loop.

This distinction matters for interpreting learning-chain evidence: a route record describes one opportunity sequence, not all potential routes and not every player's learning history. That application is an interpretation of Cook's distinction, not a source claim about this repository's data format.

### Mixtures require scale-sensitive analysis

Narrative segments, traversal, music, and gameplay can combine arcs and loops. Something consumed once at one scale may sit inside an activity repeated at a larger scale. Cook's diagnostic question is: “What repeats and what does not?” Location: **Mixing Loops and Arcs**.

A single label for an entire level or game can therefore obscure the interaction being analyzed. The relevant unit may be a recurring decision, a changing context for that decision, or information delivered alongside it.

### Authoring implications are proposals, not mandates

Cook proposes removing consumable elements as an exercise to reveal the remaining repeatable structure. He explicitly acknowledges the exercise is reductive and concludes that useful designs can mix both ingredients. Location: **Untangling loops and arcs in existing game forms**, **Conclusion**.

The essay also argues that business models influence the prevalence of consumable versus repeatable designs. Those historical and commercial claims are outside this acquisition's validation scope. They should not become requirements for teaching progression or grounds to remove narrative from a game.

## Evidence boundaries

- Complementary source, not independent corroboration: both retained essays share one author and framework lineage.
- Article offers a conceptual vocabulary; it contains no controlled experiment establishing that one player's completion demonstrates mastery or transfer.
- No basis here for deriving authored rank from loop nesting, elapsed time, physical traversal, or number of repetitions.
- Do not equate an arc with bad design, or a loop with proven engagement. Cook's conclusion calls for an appropriate mix, not a binary quality judgment.
