## Core distinctions

- **Scaffolding is an interaction process.** A prompt, model or explanation is not automatically scaffolding. The review's conceptual model combines adjustment to current understanding, subsequent withdrawal of help, and movement of responsibility toward the learner. Both participants contribute to the interaction.
- **Contingency requires information about the learner.** Support must respond to performance or understanding. Merely making help more detailed, or supplying the same sequence to everyone, does not show that support was contingent. Diagnostic activity provides the information needed for adjustment.
- **Fading is responsive rather than calendar-driven.** Help can decrease in amount or control as competence changes. The intended result is greater learner control, including cognitive, metacognitive or affective aspects of activity. Fewer interventions alone need not establish successful transfer.
- **Purpose and means differ.** The review separates what support is meant to accomplish from how it is provided. Supporting planning, managing frustration and directing attention serve different purposes. Feedback, hints, instructions, explanations, modeling and questions are possible means. The same means can serve different purposes.

## Evidence and measurement cautions

The reviewed empirical literature was dominated by small descriptive studies, especially literacy, with relatively few effectiveness studies. Rich descriptions of interaction should not be treated as a uniform causal estimate of learning gains.

Measurement can miss the construct it claims to measure. Coding teacher statements alone may miss how the learner's response changes subsequent help. A single exchange can show adjustment, while withdrawal and transfer require a longer observation window. The review notes mismatches between definitions naming all three characteristics and measures capturing only some of them.

The scaffolding metaphor also attracts criticism: a fixed building plan can obscure learner participation and differing goals. This critique matters when interpreting support as a compulsory, predetermined route.

## Repository relevance and boundary

This review helps distinguish the existence of a hint from evidence that help adapted, faded and left responsibility with the learner. Those distinctions are research vocabulary, not proof of this game's effects. No player-level diagnosis, required lesson order or automatic support policy follows from these notes.

The review discusses Wood, Bruner and Ross's original tutoring functions. That is secondary access only; these notes do not claim direct inspection of the 1976 paper.
