## Findings

Patterns can identify gameplay design choices in either a playable game or a design document. Testing a prototype can therefore reuse questions from analysis, although an intended design and an observed implementation remain distinct objects. [Source, “Using Patterns for Analysis”; “Testing”]

Methods page treats design broadly: ideas, descriptions, prototypes/mock-ups, and user tests. However, sections for ideation, conceptualization, and prototyping are placeholders. Those headings do not establish detailed author-endorsed methods for those activities. [“Using Patterns for Designing”]

A workshop observation concerns tacit knowledge. Authors report students proposing pattern candidates after learning the general concept but before seeing the collection. Students could articulate choices they already recognized and recombine those ideas. This supports a role for vocabulary in making existing knowledge discussable. It does not validate every student candidate or prove improved game quality. [“Using Patterns for Becoming Aware of Ones own Knowledge”]

Most useful qualification: pattern thinking and adopting this particular catalog are different decisions. Broad coverage is a goal of the wiki, but may make it less convenient for a narrow use case. Authors allow selecting a subset or developing another collection. This is direct support for treating catalog vocabulary as optional rather than mandatory classification. [“The Question of Orthodoxy”]
