## Authorship evidence

In section “MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research,” LeBlanc identifies Hunicke and Zubek as writers of the paper's prose and his own contribution as concepts. This is first-person background on the work, not grounds to change the paper's published three-author attribution.

## Aesthetic vocabulary has variants

The page's list uses the same eight category names as the 2004 paper. Some short descriptors differ:

- Narrative is framed as a story unfolding, rather than the paper's dramatic framing.
- Expression emphasizes communicating a viewpoint, whereas the paper emphasizes discovering oneself.
- Submission includes a low-deliberation qualifier absent from the paper's shorter pastime description.

These are observations about two retrieved versions, not proof of a chronological revision. The page has no dated change log. Do not claim either wording is a universal or exclusive definition. The distinction is useful when comparing quotations or deciding which original source a label came from. [Section: “The List”; compare paper's “Aesthetics.”]

## Workshop practice

LeBlanc describes the workshop as small-group practice: play a game, analyze its aesthetics, then alter it to meet a design constraint or address a design problem. That supports reading MDA as a tool for iterative investigation, not only a three-column classification. The page dates the workshop's start to 2001. [Section: “Game Design Workshop.”]
