## Research question and method

Article addresses a measurement problem, not merely a list of possible pleasures. Existing frameworks supply candidate motives, but do not automatically establish how many distinct dimensions exist, which are related, or how to measure them consistently.

Yee reports this development sequence:

1. Gather candidate motivations from prior game research and industry frameworks.
2. Develop approximately 50 survey items from those concepts and existing scales.
3. Pilot with 1,127 respondents, predominantly MMO players.
4. Repeat analysis with 600 respondents from a gamer panel described as more representative.
5. Ask respondents about importance of activities/elements using a 5-point response scale.
6. Use exploratory factor analysis to look for shared variation among responses and build measures around resulting groups.

This supplies provenance for an empirical approach. It does not make every inherited framework empirically equivalent or establish a representative sample of all players. The second sample is described comparatively; article text does not supply enough information to audit its representativeness here.

## Initial model and version boundary

Initial result contained 11 motivations within five groups. Crucial conceptual point: participants can vary along several dimensions at once. These are not exclusive classes into which each player must fit.

June terminology differs materially from [July v2](2015-profile-v2-source-notes.md):

- June used Strategy as a group name, with Mastery and Planning beneath it. July used Mastery as group name, with Challenge and Strategy beneath it.
- June put Customization beside Fantasy and Story under Immersion. July separated Design and Discovery into Creativity, leaving Fantasy and Story under Immersion.
- June did not yet report Discovery as its own stable motivation.

Do not mix June labels with July group structure and call the combination one published version.

## Useful qualifications in the author's account

**Exploration was initially unresolved.** Different exploratory activities associated with different dimensions: movement through places related to Fantasy, while investigation of mechanics related to Mastery. The article presents this as ongoing measurement work. Absence of an Exploration factor in June is not evidence that players never value exploration.

**Social relationships depend on the kind of competition measured.** The author contrasts competition's association with Community here against its association with Achievement in earlier MMO research. His proposed explanation concerns team-based competition versus power/domination-oriented PvP. Treat that explanation as an interpretation of changing measurement context, not a demonstrated universal causal law.

**Scope includes editorial choices.** Diversion and escapism emerged together but were left outside the framework because the authors regarded them as broader entertainment-seeking reasons rather than specifically game-content motivations. Statistical clustering alone therefore did not determine inclusion. Omission does not mean these motives are unreal or unimportant.

## Evidence limits

- This is a primary account by the model's developer, not an independent replication or a complete technical report.
- Notes do not reproduce scales, coefficients, scoring rules, or inventory items.
- Factor analysis concerns relationships among reported preferences. It does not show that adding a feature causes enjoyment.
- No responses from this repository's example game were collected. Model-development evidence does not validate that game's intended appeal.
