## Research substance

### Pure and compound estimates answer different questions

Cowan distinguishes an estimate of separately retained chunks from observed task performance supported by several mechanisms. Recall can draw on rehearsed sequences, long-term associations, sensory information, and repeated retrieval. A higher observed span does not by itself show that every item occupied one limited store simultaneously. Nor does a low score necessarily reveal storage capacity if encoding or processing demands prevented that store from being filled. [Sections 1.1–1.2.1, pp. 88–89.]

This distinction makes the paper more than a numerical correction to Miller. Cowan argues that capacity estimates become interpretable only when researchers can justify the units being counted and control other routes to successful performance. He proposes an average of about three to five chunks under those restricted conditions, not a task-independent ceiling on all meaningful activity. [Sections 1 and 5, pp. 87–90, 114.]

### Four routes to estimating capacity

The review groups evidence into four approaches: overload presentation so broad recoding cannot occur; specifically block recoding and rehearsal; inspect changes in performance as set size increases; and examine indirect signatures such as grouping or sequential effects. These are research strategies with assumptions, not interchangeable tests that automatically diagnose capacity. [Section 1.2, pp. 88–89; Table 1, p. 90.]

A persistent visible array complicates such inference because a person can return to the display and use storage repeatedly. Conversely, an overly brief or masked presentation can underestimate capacity by limiting acquisition. Tasks requiring continual updating can add demands beyond retaining the nominal target items. [Section 1.2.1, p. 89.]

### Chunk identity requires evidence

Cowan defines chunks through stronger associations within a group than between concurrently used groups. Familiar abbreviations illustrate the problem: one person's recognized letter group can remain several unrelated letters for another person. Even familiar abbreviations may share associations with one another, invalidating a simple count. [Section 1.3, pp. 89–90.]

Possible evidence includes response timing, association patterns, and reports of grouping. Cowan acknowledges that many studies reviewed did not directly measure chunking; his interpretation often infers chunk structure from task conditions. The proposed convergence therefore depends partly on those eligibility assumptions. [Section 1.3, p. 90.]

### Attention can shift across available information

Cowan's proposed framework locates the capacity constraint in a focus of attention. Other temporarily accessible information can supply that focus over time. The focus can shift between levels of organization or types of material; successful performance need not imply simultaneous awareness of the entire representation. [Sections 2–2.7, pp. 91–93; conclusion, p. 114.]

Long-term knowledge can support larger meaningful patterns. Thus expert advantage can concern the size and organization of chunks rather than an increase in their count. This is a theoretical interpretation informed by prior work, not direct evidence about novice and expert players in a supplied game. [Section 2.2, p. 92.]

## Evidence boundaries and disagreement

- Distinguish Cowan's model assumptions from established observations. A single central focus is the account argued for, not an uncontested mechanism.
- Opening of author response reports substantial disagreement among commentators about both existence and explanation of a common limit. [Author response, R1, p. 154.]
- Conclusion explicitly requires identifiable chunks and restricted reuse of storage. It also acknowledges individual variation. [Section 5, p. 114.]
- Do not convert four chunks into four UI controls, four rules, four prerequisites, or four tolerated errors.
- Visible supports change the available information and opportunities for resampling. This paper does not decide whether those supports preserve a particular intended challenge.
- No player-capacity dataset, game-specific effect size, or evidence of the example game's player experience is supplied here.
