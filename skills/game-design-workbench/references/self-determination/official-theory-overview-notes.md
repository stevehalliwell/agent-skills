## Structure of the framework

The overview presents SDT as broader than a three-item checklist. It concerns the quality and regulation of motivation, social conditions supporting or obstructing development, and well-being. Its organismic account assumes an active person whose tendencies toward learning and integration depend partly on surrounding support.

Autonomy, competence, and relatedness recur across the framework, but different questions belong to different components:

1. **Cognitive Evaluation Theory:** conditions affecting intrinsic interest, including how feedback, rewards, and interpersonal control influence autonomy and competence.
2. **Organismic Integration Theory:** how externally prompted behavior can remain pressured or become personally accepted. Extrinsic motivation varies in autonomy; it is not uniformly coercive.
3. **Causality Orientations Theory:** individual differences in how people orient toward situations and regulate action. This is distinct from assuming that a game feature reveals a stable player type.
4. **Basic Psychological Needs Theory:** links among need satisfaction, need obstruction, functioning, and wellness across settings and cultures.
5. **Goal Contents Theory:** differences in what people pursue and how those aims afford need satisfaction. Goal content is not the same question as whether regulation is autonomous.
6. **Relationships Motivation Theory:** quality of close relationships and group belonging, including how relationships support more than relatedness alone.

This division helps distinguish questions about interest in an activity, reasons for continuing it, goals pursued through it, and relationships around it. Treating all four as a single measure of “fun” loses information.

## Useful distinctions and limits

- Need support and need thwarting describe environmental and experiential relations. Reading a rule does not establish what a participant felt.
- The overview's universality claims concern psychological needs across contexts. They do not imply that every brief game encounter must independently supply all forms of life support.
- Connections between support and persistence are theoretical and research claims, not evidence that any observed persistence has a particular cause.
- The overview spans education, work, health, relationships, media, and other domains. Claims about a specific game still require game- and player-level evidence.
