## Purpose and locus

Provisional application guide; human source/completeness review remains pending. Source model comes from Sweller (1988) and Sweller, van Merriënboer and Paas (2019). Slice tracing, review artifacts and game-design questions below are workbench adaptations, not a published game-evaluation instrument.

Use cognitive load theory to investigate how learning a particular relationship competes with searching, remembering and interpreting information. Ask whether a player can construct knowledge usable beyond the current solution, not merely reach the exit. Useful targets include a first encounter with coupled rules, a demonstration, a hint, or a short sequence combining previously introduced operations.

**Unit of analysis:** a learner processing particular information through a particular task and presentation, with specified prior knowledge. Neither a mechanic in isolation nor a whole game's prerequisite count has a fixed cognitive cost. Trace one moment's interacting information, then enough surrounding practice to ask what could be learned and transferred.

**Intended use:** generate bounded explanations and support variants for unfamiliar coordination. Compare them with supplied concept/experience intentions, or explore candidate consequences in `game-explorer` mode. Deliberate uncertainty or search can be part of play; reducing all effort is not the goal.

**Non-use:** no universal difficulty rating, maximum mechanic count, inferred capability/mastery/emotion, clinical diagnosis, aggregate psychological/fun score, or proof of enjoyment. This learning lens does not replace Flow, memory-specific analysis, or individual MDA aesthetics. Concept/player experience remains broader than an MDA aesthetic record. Selection of this guide authorizes neither design adoption nor record edits; no automatic framework sweep follows.

## Architecture: why knowledge changes demand

Both sources centre domain-specific knowledge, not a generic problem-solving talent.

- **Working memory** handles novel information with limited capacity and duration. Processing relationships matters as well as retaining items. A display may leave everything visible yet still require difficult integration.
- **Long-term memory** stores organised knowledge. In the 1988 account, a **schema** lets someone recognise a class of problem states and associate it with useful moves. Remembering an isolated answer is not the same as recognising that relationship elsewhere.
- **Acquisition and automation** change later processing. Several initially separate elements can become one familiar organised unit; recurrent operations can require less deliberate processing. The 2019 claim that familiar knowledge escapes ordinary working-memory limits concerns access to organised long-term knowledge, not unlimited ability to handle arbitrary new rules.
- **Element interactivity** means elements must be considered together to understand or perform the task. Independent facts can be learned separately; interacting constraints cannot be understood merely by memorising each in isolation. Which things function as elements depends on the learner's knowledge. A familiar rule combination may function as one unit for one player and several unfamiliar relations for another.

Thus, distinguish **sequence** from **coordination**. Three operations across a room are not necessarily three concurrently processed elements. Conversely, a single push could require relating legal destination, future access, switch occupancy and remaining energy. That is a hypothetical task analysis, not a measured element count or a fact about the example room.

The 2019 synthesis adds an evolutionary account. It distinguishes biologically primary knowledge, acquired through evolved capacities such as ordinary speech, from culturally specific secondary knowledge such as reading or particular formal procedures. Its instructional focus is effortful acquisition of domain-specific secondary knowledge. Do not turn this distinction into a classification of players or claim that a game trains general intelligence.

Five principles explain the synthesis's architecture:

1. **Information store:** long-term memory holds the knowledge needed to function.
2. **Borrowing and reorganising:** much knowledge comes from others, making explicit explanation and examples plausible routes to learning.
3. **Randomness as genesis:** without relevant knowledge to retrieve or borrow, generate-and-test search supplies candidate moves.
4. **Narrow limits of change:** only limited novel information can be processed at once; the review also considers capacity variation after sustained effort.
5. **Environmental organising and linking:** environmental cues activate organised knowledge and guide action.

These principles link presentation, search and knowledge acquisition. They do not make every game demonstration effective or every exploratory move wasteful. Prior knowledge and learning outcomes still need evidence.

## Load distinctions and the changed account

Do not use three independent fixed workload buckets. The 2019 review explicitly revises the earlier three-category account it attributes to 1998; the 1988 paper is not a presentation of that later taxonomy.

**Intrinsic load:** demand arising from the information relationships to be learned, relative to current knowledge. It changes if the learning target changes or the learner acquires relevant knowledge. A simpler introductory task may manage intrinsic demand by temporarily changing what must be coordinated; it does not make the original relationship inherently simpler. Eventually inspect reintegration and independent use.

**Extraneous load:** demand from presentation or required procedures that do not serve the learning target. Searching between mutually dependent displays or maintaining unnecessary subgoals can add interacting elements. In the revised account, element interactivity affects extraneous as well as intrinsic load. The distinction is not “rules versus interface”: a tutorial procedure can add burden, and interpreting an interface may itself be part of the intended learning task.

**Germane processing/load:** working-memory resources devoted to dealing with intrinsic information for learning. In the 2019 formulation this is a redistribution of resources toward learning-relevant activity, **not an additional independent load added to intrinsic plus extraneous load**. Reducing extraneous demand can reduce total load and leave more resources available for learning. It does not guarantee those resources will be invested in learning.

The review explains why the earlier additive account changed: if germane load always replaced reduced extraneous load, total load should stay unchanged, yet studies reported lower load after extraneous demand fell. Some historical instructional-effect passages still describe “increasing germane load.” Read those alongside the explicit revision and measurement section, not as a second formula to calculate.

Classification depends on the target. Remembering an unseen condition could be incidental to learning spatial causality, but central to a deliberately selected memory task. Establish intention before calling it extraneous. Even then, intention alone does not establish successful learning or justify every burden. Unknown presentation or prior knowledge leaves classification conditional.

## What the two sources establish

### 1988: search can compete with schema acquisition

Means–ends analysis compares current state with a goal, selects operators to reduce differences, and may maintain subgoals. Successful search can direct attention toward reaching that goal without attending to reusable state–move relationships. It can also consume processing resources needed to acquire those relationships. Forward movement alone does not identify a schema: novices can work forward through search or exploration too.

Sweller compares conventional and nonspecific-goal equation-chain problems using a deliberately minimal production-system model. Figures 1–2 contrast goal/subgoal branching with repeatedly solving any available unknown. Table 2 compares model statements, active productions, cycles and matched conditions. These are model-dependent indicators, not a conversion from game mechanics to human workload. The model excludes translation into the initial representation and does not implement a complete learning process.

The article also reports a dual-task experiment with 24 Year 10 students, previously introduced to trigonometric ratios. Two groups solved conventional or nonspecific-goal problems and reproduced the preceding problem's givens and solution. The primary task showed no significant overall group difference in solution time or mathematical errors; the nonspecific-goal group made fewer reproduction errors on several measures, including structural positions and solutions. Recall was used as evidence relevant to schema acquisition, not its complete definition. This experiment was not a direct test of later independent problem-solving transfer; the article discusses earlier studies separately.

Practical implication: completion alone can conceal differences in what was retained. Do not expand the result into “puzzles prevent learning” or “remove all goals.” The paper notes that undirected search is impractical in large problem spaces; its goal-free argument relies on bounded tasks.

### 2019: a broader, conditional instructional account

The synthesis relates architecture, revised load categories, instructional effects and measurement. It extends beyond one search comparison to guidance, presentation, practice and changing expertise. Its **compound effects** qualify other effects rather than add universal prescriptions: material interactivity, expertise and information transience can remove or reverse an instructional advantage.

The related **4C/ID** model concerns longer courses, not a mandatory game progression schema. Figure 1 distinguishes:

- Whole **learning tasks** coordinating recurrent routines and non-recurrent reasoning, arranged by increasing complexity, with varied practice and fading support within each complexity level.
- **Supportive information** explaining domain organisation and reasoning approaches, studied apart from performing the demanding task.
- **Procedural information** giving just-in-time how-to guidance and corrective feedback for recurrent aspects.
- **Part-task practice** automating selected recurrent aspects, not replacing whole-task coordination.

In the figure, support can increase again when task complexity rises; fading is not one irreversible march toward no help. Workbench adaptation: examine whether a sequence teaches isolated operations but never supports their combination. This does not require adopting 4C/ID, inferring automation from a prerequisite link, or filling four new record categories.

## Instructional effects as conditional options

Select an option only when its mechanism matches the suspected problem. These are source-derived distinctions; hypothetical game uses require local evaluation.

- **Goal-free tasks:** reduce goal/subgoal search so learners can examine available relations. Consider a bounded exploratory exercise, not deletion of a game's purpose. Large search spaces can defeat the rationale.
- **Worked examples and completion problems:** show a full solution and its state–move relationships, or leave part for the learner to complete. A demonstration that is skipped or merely copied need not teach the relation. Partial completion can bridge toward independent solving.
- **Split attention versus redundancy:** integrate complementary information that must be combined to make sense; do not assume that adding a second self-contained explanation helps. The latter can impose redundant processing. Neither effect means “all text is bad.”
- **Modality and transient information:** narration alongside a diagram can distribute processing, but long disappearing narration may require retention and reverse the benefit. Replay, stable information, pacing and segmentation are alternatives; audio is not a universal replacement for accessible text.
- **Expertise reversal and guidance fading:** help that benefits novices can become redundant or counterproductive after relevant knowledge grows. Consider optional or reduced support and test unaided use; time spent or authored rank does not certify expertise.
- **Isolated elements and simple-to-complex sequencing:** learn component information before integrating overwhelming relationships. Reintroduce those relationships; permanent isolation cannot establish coordinated understanding.
- **Variability and self-explanation:** contrasting cases or explaining why a move works may promote reusable knowledge while increasing processing demand. More reflection is not always better when the task already overwhelms available resources.
- **Imagination, collaboration and human movement:** mental rehearsal requires enough knowledge to represent the task; collaboration trades distributed processing against communication costs; observed human movement has specific instructional evidence. None supports assuming imagined solutions, multiplayer play or animated crate motion automatically improves learning.

Roles: `skill-atoms`, `challenges`, `mechanics`, `experiments`.

Inspection topics: skill atoms, challenge routes, and relevant source records.

Read selected records and their actual presentation/timing context. Use concept/mode for purpose; use prerequisite and challenge-role links as authored learning intentions, not observed knowledge. Where relevant, inspect interaction feedback, resource rules and `control-vars` by their existing owners. A configuration value is not a psychological measurement.

Needed inputs are a bounded task, intended relationship to learn, supplied familiarity evidence or explicit assumptions, available information/cues, action sequence, assistance and any actual observations. Missing layout or build evidence limits tracing; it does not authorize inventing a room. Consult only relevant records; no compulsory category filling or new required schema.

Preserve all 14 existing roles. `experiments` own questions, hypotheses, local measures, methods, observations and interpretation. No separate measures, decisions or skills collection. ## Apply to one design slice

1. **Bound the learning question.** Name encounter/phase and relationship that should remain usable later. State current concept/mode and whether the target is supplied or hypothetical. Done: success means more than “finished the room,” or the lack of a learning target is explicit.
2. **Trace task and presentation.** Describe what the player sees, must relate, searches for, retains, chooses and receives as feedback. Separate operations occurring sequentially from information needing joint consideration. Done: cite evidence for each critical relation; mark missing layout/timing unknown.
3. **State knowledge dependence.** Identify assumed familiar units versus novel relations. Cite relevant prior-task opportunities and actual observations separately. Done: no prerequisite link, genre label or authored rank substitutes for demonstrated knowledge.
4. **Form a demand explanation.** Identify the learning-relevant relation and one suspected competing procedure/presentation demand. Use intrinsic/extraneous distinctions conditionally; describe germane processing as engagement with the relation. Done: explain mechanism without a numerical load budget, and name an alternative cause such as unclear feedback or an input error.
5. **Choose one discriminating variant.** Keep the intended decision/relationship stable where possible; vary the suspected burden. If simplifying changes the learning target, say so and specify later reintegration. Done: variant remains proposed, with a counterprediction or circumstance where it could hurt.
6. **Specify evidence needed.** Separate immediate performance, account of the relationship, and later unaided use in a changed case. A changed case should require the same relation without permitting rote move copying. Done: state what would weaken the explanation and which confounds remain. Any authorized study's measures and observations belong in an experiment, not new review fields imposed on source records.
7. **Return a bounded artifact.** Include source facts, assumptions, demand hypothesis, alternative, variant, evidence plan and unresolved limits. Done: no inferred player state, executed trial, adoption or record mutation is implied.

## Evidence and measurement limits

**Performance is not load, and load is not learning.** Long attempts, errors, restarts or fast solutions each admit several explanations. A copied sequence may succeed without reusable knowledge; demanding practice may support learning. Seek converging task, presentation and learning evidence rather than assigning a category from telemetry alone.

The 1988 computational comparison is a constrained model of strategies, not a native workbench computation. Its reproduction task is an indicator relevant to structural learning, not a mastery test to transplant unchanged into games.

The 2019 measurement review distinguishes:

- **Subjective mental-effort ratings:** practical overall indicators, but not straightforward separation of intrinsic and extraneous load. Wording, scale changes, timing and frequency matter. A single end-of-session rating can differ from an average of ratings after tasks; do not equate effort with task difficulty or invent a validated local scale.
- **Multi-indicator questionnaires:** promising differentiation of intrinsic/extraneous demand, with more validation needed across questions and domains. This guide reproduces no proprietary instrument and supplies no substitute scoring key.
- **Secondary tasks:** performance may indicate spare processing capacity, but the task itself can interfere with play and cannot simply separate load types.
- **Physiological measures:** EEG and eye measures require interpretation and appropriate methods. A pupil change is not a direct category label or a causal diagnosis; the review notes population-dependent results and unresolved validity questions.

A local experiment must define its own operational measures, context, timing and undefined cases. Combine any effort report with immediate and later learning outcomes appropriate to the question. Do not combine these into an aggregate psychological/fun score. No participant protocol or experiment execution is authorized by applying this guide.

## Questions and common mistakes

- **What relationship should survive a changed layout?** If no answer is supplied, do not optimise an invented curriculum. Separate learning support from intended puzzle experience.
- **Which information must meet in the same decision?** A long prerequisite history is not simultaneous demand; a short action can conceal substantial coordination.
- **What would make this relation familiar?** Record exposure opportunities separately from evidence of retrieval and independent use. Do not infer expertise from challenge completion alone.
- **What does the cue actually replace?** It might remove searching, remembering or interpretation; those are different explanations. Adding a redundant overlay could increase rather than reduce demand.
- **Would the same help still be useful after learning?** Check redundancy and expertise reversal before prescribing permanent guidance. Do not infer the point to fade support from elapsed time alone.
- **Did the variant reveal the answer or expose the relation?** Completion gains from answer leakage do not establish schema acquisition. Compare changed cases, not just repetitions of the taught move sequence.
- **Did reduced task complexity remove the point of play?** Intrinsic demand is not a defect. Restore coordination after component practice and compare with supplied intentions before recommending simplification.
- **What would falsify this explanation?** If the condition stays visible, players explain it correctly, or a variant changes several rules at once, narrow or withdraw the original claim rather than calling every failure overload.

## Source and transfer limits

Both actual local articles were read, including their main-text conclusions and bibliography; exported figures were inspected. The former abstract-only/full-study-not-read statement is superseded. Books and other bibliography-only leads were not acquired or independently read; findings cited by the 2019 synthesis remain synthesis-level evidence here. No framework-completeness or catalog-readiness claim follows.

The 1988 source contains a small school trigonometry experiment, a minimal equation-chain model and discussion of earlier work. It does not validate a human-load calculator for spatial games. The 2019 source is a historical/theoretical research synthesis, not a new controlled game study or an unchanged 1988 model. Its future directions on depletion, self-regulation, stress/emotion/uncertainty and movement include open questions and theoretical extensions; do not infer these states from authored game tension or observed failure. Its own discussion makes task purpose relevant to whether uncertainty is intrinsic or extraneous.

Conversion limits remain: 1988 sentence/line order, tables and equations contain defects; 2019 Table 1 has merged associations and typography artifacts. This guide uses surrounding explanatory prose rather than reconstructing damaged table rows or quoting equations. The three exported 1988 figures and supplemented 2019 Figure 1 were available and inspected; other 2019 placeholders are not additional verified visual content. Original page layout/PDF fidelity was not checked in this offline authoring pass.

Game application remains a transfer hypothesis. Educational learning outcomes do not by themselves establish enjoyment, voluntary engagement, accessibility or the intended player experience. Human review should check source interpretation, whether this bounded adaptation fits actual presentation, and which unknowns need investigation—not fill every category or adopt every support option.
