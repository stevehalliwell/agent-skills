## Scope and source boundary

Inspect a selected action/target pair: what happens under particular conditions, how the player could perceive the outcome, and what that outcome means within further play. Useful for an ambiguous interaction, a blocked attempt, feedback that seems clear but strategically uninformative, or a consequence whose later significance is uncertain.

The inspected chapter explains meaningful play through player action, system response, and context. It distinguishes descriptive and evaluative meanings, discernible and integrated outcomes, and formal/social/cultural significance. It does **not** prescribe this workbench's nouns, verbs, schemas, matrix, coverage states, or inspection procedure. Do not attribute those modeling choices to the authors or treat this chapter as the whole book.

**Review draft:** chapter-backed expansion, provisional for human review. Procedure and examples below are original applications, not a reproduced source instrument. This guide authorizes selected interpretation and proposals, not automatic record repair, playtesting, goal adoption, or an all-framework sweep.

## Meaningful play: two senses

Source: Two Kinds of Meaningful Play, printed pp. 33–34; summary p. 37.

- **Descriptive:** meaning arises in the relation between a player's action and the system's response. Inspect the relation rather than describe an isolated input or asset. In the chapter's Gross-Out example, a story's role in play also depends on other players' responses; narrative content alone does not establish its game meaning.
- **Evaluative:** the authors judge action/outcome relationships through whether they are discernible and integrated into the larger game. This is their proposed design aim, not a numerical quality score, moral judgment, or empirical claim that all players value the same consequence.

Rules and objects alone do not establish the experienced meaning. Meaning and Play (pp. 32–33) places interaction within player and play context. The later discussion (p. 36) includes strategic, social, and cultural meanings. A matrix can help expose formal relationships without exhaustively modeling conversation, performance, spectatorship, cultural context, or a player's interpretation.

Keep **defined consequence**, **communicated consequence**, **understood significance**, and **felt response** distinct. A documented rule can establish the first without establishing the others. When the question concerns a specific feeling, use the [shared vocabulary](../references/emotional-experience/emotions.md) to name it; understanding a rule does not prove satisfaction, relief, frustration, or any other feeling. No separate emotion review happens automatically.

## Discernibility and integration

Source: Discernable and Integrated, pp. 34–35. The chapter spells the term *discernable*; this guide also uses the common *discernible* spelling for the same source concept.

**Discernibility:** can the player perceive the action's result? Examine actual available cues, where/when they occur, and what they distinguish. Source examples include off-screen attacks, uncommunicated hunger, and shooting an asteroid: consequential events can occur without adequate communication. These are illustrations, not evidence that a particular presentation always irritates or informs every player.

A visible change may establish that *something* happened without explaining its relevant condition or cause. Conversely, a defined no-effect state outcome is not an empty rule. A blocked action can still raise questions about acknowledgment, reason, and available alternatives. This is a workbench application, not a claim that the chapter prescribes a special cue for every failed input.

**Integration:** how does the outcome connect to later choices and the larger activity? Source examples contrast a footrace disconnected from the larger competition with one contributing to its score, and relate chess openings to later play. Inspect changed options, commitments, resources, relationships, or interpretation—not only points or victory progress.

The chapter also allows delayed significance: an early narrative action may become fully understandable only at the end. Do not turn discernibility into a requirement to reveal every hidden rule, future consequence, mystery, or plot outcome immediately. Distinguish intentional uncertainty from an unexamined information gap, and name the intended point of recognition. A delayed payoff remains a hypothesis until the relevant experience is examined.

The two questions are related but not interchangeable:

- A clear flash can make an event perceptible while leaving its later relevance unclear.
- A consequence can matter mechanically while remaining hard to perceive or interpret.
- A deliberately inconsequential choice can serve expression, playfulness, pacing, or another supplied purpose; missing strategic leverage alone does not justify removal.

## Workbench grammar and case ownership

These are workbench conventions, not an extracted book ontology.

- **Identity/action:** nouns identify applicable targets; verbs identify actions. Resolve canonical terms and declared aliases. A wall can constrain pushing a crate without being the target of that push.
- **Conditional case:** one target/action pair can have several cases, each with conditions and consequences. Shared mechanics own reusable rules; cases own their particular conditions/consequences/feedback and link to shared behavior rather than copy it.
- **Coverage versus lifecycle:** `defined`, `unknown`, `excluded`, and `not_applicable` describe coverage. Accepted/proposed/rejected status describes authority or disposition. An accepted unknown case is still unresolved, not a defined outcome.
- **No-effect outcome:** an explicit applicable case can change neither position nor resource balance. Keep it distinct from an operation declared not applicable, a rejected proposal, or an absent record.
- **Overlapping conditions:** compare their actual outcomes before alleging conflict. Two conditions can both forbid displacement without disagreeing. Precedence matters only where the supplied outcomes or presentation compete; do not invent it or require a redundant joint case automatically.
- **Sparse matrix:** one cell can contain several cases. Use explicitly declared applicable nouns, not every contextual noun or incidental prose mention. A blank cell does not establish an impossible, prohibited, or defective interaction.

## Apply to a selected slice

1. **Bound the question.** Select one pair, decision, or small supplied set and its play context. Resolve identity/aliases and authority. Done: target, action, relevant conditions, and inspected scope are explicit; no full Cartesian review is implied.
2. **Recover the actual cases.** Read each relevant case and its shared mechanic. Separate changed state, unchanged state, excluded operations, unknowns, and presentation. Done: every claimed outcome cites its owner or remains unresolved.
3. **Compare conditions and outcomes.** Check overlaps, conflicting consequences, and gaps that matter to the selected question. Consider whether the combination is even established as reachable. Done: genuine conflict, compatible overlap, and unknown reachability are not conflated; no silent rule ordering or repair.
4. **Examine discernibility.** State what the player could perceive and what that cue does not yet establish. Include acknowledgment/reason only as relevant questions, not compulsory effects. Done: declared presentation, assumed comprehension, and observation are separate.
5. **Trace integration.** Follow at least one relevant later choice, state, relationship, or intended delayed recognition. Compare with the supplied purpose; name an alternative interpretation. Done: significance is explained, or its missing basis is explicit, without prescribing a new reward or goal.
6. **Return a bounded comparison.** Give source-backed facts, interpretation, counterreading, unresolved question, and useful evidence if needed. Done: designer can inspect the finding without adopting a rule, filling every cell, saving a report, or running a study.

Stop with a useful partial account when input is missing. Report saving, record changes, and investigations require the corresponding user request; a selected review does not authorize them implicitly.

## Worked example: pushing a crate

Sources: [free push](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/interactions/free-push.md), [wall-blocked push](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/interactions/wall-blocked-push.md), [low-energy push](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/interactions/low-energy-push.md), [shared pushing rule](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/mechanics/crate-pushing.md), [occupied switch](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/interactions/occupied-switch.md), and [switch/exit rule](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/mechanics/switch-exit.md).

**Question:** can a player distinguish a failed push from a successful but strategically unhelpful push, and understand why either matters?

| Case | Authored consequence | Declared presentation | Discernibility/integration question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free destination and enough energy | Move crate one cell, then spend push-cost. | Free-push text says displacement is visible; energy-display feedback is not established there. | Does the person notice spending and connect the move to a later useful placement? Visible movement alone does not establish either understanding. |
| Destination contains a wall | No movement and no spending. | Additional blocked-action feedback explicitly unspecified. | Does the person recognize the attempt and relevant obstruction, or interpret no change another way? Defined state behavior is not proof of a clear cue. |
| Energy below push-cost | No displacement and no spending. | No distinguishing low-energy feedback declared in the case. | Can the person distinguish resource insufficiency from obstruction or an unrecognized input? Do not invent a sound, message, or energy penalty. |

**Compatible overlap:** wall blockage and low energy can both describe why a push fails if that combination occurs. The shared state outcomes agree: no movement/spending. There is no demonstrated state-rule contradiction. Which reason is communicated remains unknown; distinct presentation or reachable geometry is not supplied.

**Possible integration:** moving a crate onto the switch opens the exit; removing it closes the exit. That connects placement to later exit state, not merely to local animation. Occupied-switch text says exit state is visible but leaves sound unspecified. Whether a particular push can reach a switch in a particular level, whether the player recognizes the connection, and how the player feels are not established by these records. No physical map or play observations supplied.

**Counterreading:** an unsuccessful attempt might teach a boundary, be ignored, or feel confusing. A successful move might be helpful, waste energy, or need later reconsideration. Do not confuse failed attempts, which cost nothing here, with successful moves that spend energy. [Careful positioning](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/dynamics/careful-positioning.md) is a prediction, not an observed result.

**Other coverage states:** [wall activation](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/interactions/wall-activation.md) is explicitly not applicable, not a no-effect activation rule. [Closed-exit walking](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/interactions/closed-exit-walk.md) is explicitly unknown, not a prohibition. [Restart](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/mechanics/challenge-restart.md) is documented; missing challenge links do not prove it is unavailable. None of these requires filling every matrix cell.

**Useful next question:** which distinction does the designer want the player to perceive at the failed attempt, and when should placement's later significance become clear? This is a possible review question, not adoption of feedback changes or authorization to test them.

## Original prompts

- What is the action's actual target, and which objects only constrain its outcome?
- Which condition selects each consequence? Do overlapping conditions conflict, agree, or remain untested for reachability?
- What lets the person perceive the result? What would distinguish another plausible explanation?
- What later choice, commitment, relationship, or interpretation gives this result significance?
- Is the intended significance immediate, cumulative, retrospective, social, or deliberately unresolved?
- Does the player's possible interpretation differ from what the designer knows? Which missing context matters?
- Is the review judging an action/outcome relation, or merely counting effects, rewards, covered cells, or visible animations?
- What supplied purpose explains a deliberately low-stakes, ambiguous, or non-strategic action?

## Evidence and counterchecks

A written case can establish intended behavior, not observed comprehension or experienced meaning. A runnable prototype can establish implemented response under tested conditions, not automatically the reason someone understood it. No build or playtest is executed here.

If an investigation is requested, distinguish the actual event, available cue, person's account, and their later action. Explaining an outcome after repeated trials differs from recognizing its significance on a first encounter. Correctly naming a rule need not establish emotional response, good pacing, or transfer to another situation. State consent/context and sample limits where relevant.

Countercheck preferred explanations against another plausible cause: a visible wall, prior genre knowledge, hidden input failure, depleted energy, accidental success, or unclear goals. These are hypotheses, not added game facts. Repeated attempts or a facial expression do not uniquely identify confusion, frustration, curiosity, or motive.

Source examples and the authors' evaluative criterion do not supply validated thresholds or automatic defect severity. Intentional delayed recognition can differ from missing feedback. Formal integration can coexist with social meanings not encoded in the records. A sparse declared model remains useful without proving exhaustive meaning, playability, or audience fit.

## Common traps and missing data

- **Book-prescribed matrix:** attribute action/outcome and discernible/integrated concepts to the chapter; attribute matrix and case contracts to the workbench.
- **Defined means understood:** a rule, a presentation claim, and actual player interpretation are different evidence.
- **Overlap means contradiction:** inspect consequences before requiring precedence. Compatible no-effect cases need not become competing rules.
- **Every action must award something:** integration concerns larger significance, not mandatory points, state mutation, progress, or immediate positive feedback.
- **All future consequences must be disclosed:** preserve a supplied mystery or delayed recognition; inspect its intended meaning rather than automatically remove it.
- **Missing case means forbidden:** keep absence, explicit unknown, deliberate exclusion, and not-applicable distinct. Preserve rejected proposals and their rationale; do not reactivate them to fill cells.
- **Feedback exists because genre suggests it:** unspecified sound, animation, input acknowledgment, and resource display stay unspecified.
- **Coverage proves reachability or quality:** record links/counts show declared structure. No supplied level geometry means no proof of spatial arrangements, route feasibility, or witnessed comprehension.
