## Purpose and locus

Use this lens when a selected design question concerns what help makes possible, how help responds to a learner, and which decisions the learner later takes over. Useful slices include an optional hint exchange, a demonstration followed by practice, or assistance across a short challenge route. Return a support hypothesis with evidence limits, not a verdict that a tutorial teaches successfully.

Keep two units of analysis distinct:

- **ZPD in Chaiklin's interpretation:** whole child's psychological development in a social and historical situation. Focus is changing relations among psychological functions across developmental periods, not the difficulty of one puzzle or the distance between two task scores.
- **Scaffolding in the 2010 review:** teacher–student interaction over time. Focus is responsive help, its withdrawal, and learner responsibility. A single hint describes a possible means; a sequence of responses and later activity is needed to assess the process.

**Workbench adaptation:** inspect one learner–task–support relationship and its later independent opportunity. Use the scaffolding vocabulary for game-specific learning unless a defensible developmental theory and appropriate evidence support a narrower ZPD claim. Do not call the space between easy and hard challenges a measured ZPD.

Do not use this lens to infer player capability, mastery, developmental level, emotion, or an optimal challenge curve. It does not prescribe compulsory teaching order, automatic difficulty adjustment, or a hint algorithm. It is not an enjoyment score. Compare proposals with the concept's overall intended experience; an individual MDA aesthetic is not a substitute for that intent. A design can favor discovery over efficient instruction without thereby failing this lens.

## ZPD is a developmental relation

Chaiklin reconstructs ZPD within Vygotsky's theory rather than treating the familiar independent/assisted definition as sufficient. His critique targets three common assumptions:

- **Generality:** any skill acquisition counts as development. In his reading, learning a technical task and qualitative psychological development are not identical.
- **Assistance:** more competent help defines the zone. Assistance matters because of what it reveals about developing functions, not simply because someone completes more tasks with help.
- **Potential:** the zone is a fixed learner property or a place of easy, joyful learning. Neither follows. Potential concerns currently maturing functions in a developmental relation; learning need not be effortless or pleasant.

The model's components explain why those restrictions matter:

1. **Psychological structure:** relations among functions such as perception, speech, thinking, and voluntary memory. Development changes their organization, not merely the count of solved tasks.
2. **Social situation of development:** child's particular relation to social/material environment, including needs, interests, demands, and possibilities. Child actively engages with this situation rather than passively receiving instruction.
3. **Age period, leading activity, and central new-formation:** historically constituted developmental periods, not just chronological brackets. A leading activity organizes actions relevant to a central contradiction; resulting new organization helps bring a transition to another period. These are not game levels, prerequisite ranks, or designer-selected learning objectives.
4. **Objective zone:** Chaiklin's name for the relation among present period, functions maturing within it, and next period. “Objective” refers to a historically constituted developmental demand, not an objective difficulty metric.
5. **Subjective zone:** an individual child's current position relative to those maturing functions and that objective zone. “Subjective” does not mean player preference or self-report. Neither zone resides solely inside the child.

Collaboration can reveal maturing functions that independent performance does not reveal. Chaiklin explains Vygotsky's technical use of **imitation** as participation with some understanding of structural relations, not blind copying. Demonstration, partial solution, or leading question can provide an opportunity to investigate that relation. Assisted success alone does not explain its developmental meaning; task indicators require interpretation within a theory.

Chaiklin also identifies unfinished procedural and theoretical work. His account does not supply a standardized intervention sequence or a reliable game-player diagnostic. In this guide, “independent attempt,” “assisted completion,” and “later independent application” remain bounded descriptions, not estimates of a subjective zone.

## Scaffolding is an interactive process

Van de Pol, Volman and Beishuizen (2010) synthesize three connected characteristics. Their Figure 1 depicts repeated response/support cycles at two times, with teacher support shrinking and student responsibility growing between them. Diagnostic strategies and support strategies both sit inside the interaction. It is not a one-way staircase of ever smaller hints.

- **Contingency:** adapt support to current demonstrated performance. Find out what the learner is doing or understanding, check that interpretation, and respond. A high-detail explanation can be contingent in one exchange and excessive in another. A small hint can be responsive or insufficient. Support size alone does not establish contingency.
- **Fading:** decrease amount and/or level of support over time as the learner can take on more. A timer that removes hints regardless of responses is withdrawal, not demonstrated contingent fading. New subject matter may require renewed support; reinstatement is not automatically failure.
- **Transfer of responsibility:** learner takes increasing control of relevant activity. This may concern reasoning, planning/monitoring, or affect-related regulation in the source model. Help disappearing does not prove responsibility transferred: learner may be stuck, disengaged, or relying on another source of help.

The review treats questioning, modeling, and other means as scaffolding only when embedded in a process with these characteristics. For a static design document, use “proposed scaffold” or “support opportunity”; do not certify an interaction that has not occurred.

The **contingent-shift principle**, discussed in both articles through later tutoring research, increases instructional control following demonstrated difficulty and decreases it following success. Here control means how much of the task the tutor regulates, not workbench `control-vars`. This principle is an analytic reference, not permission to map every failed move to a stronger hint. A failed game action may be deliberate testing; successful execution may be copying.

Keep **transfer of responsibility** separate from **transfer to another task**. The former concerns who regulates current work. The latter concerns later use in changed circumstances. Both matter to a game learning proposal, but neither proves the other.

## Distinguish intention from means

The 2010 review separates **what the support is for** from **how support is offered**. A strategy combines an intention and a means; no combination guarantees learning. Classification also depends on how the learner responds, not wording alone.

Five intentions appear as A–E in Table 1:

- **Direction maintenance:** keep activity oriented toward a goal; chiefly supports planning/monitoring. A hypothetical prompt to reconsider the current objective differs from telling a player the next move.
- **Cognitive structuring:** supply an explanatory organization or relation that makes information meaningful. A hypothetical explanation linking two state changes targets understanding, not just attention.
- **Reduction of degrees of freedom:** take over part of a task so the learner handles a smaller remainder. State which decision is removed and which remains. Simplification can also remove the very reasoning under investigation.
- **Recruitment:** draw learner into a task and its requirements. This is an intended function, not proof of interest.
- **Contingency management/frustration control:** source category combines performance incentives/consequences and support intended to prevent or reduce frustration. It does not authorize punishment, assumed emotion, or rewards in a game. “Contingency management” here is not the same term as responsive support contingency.

Six means describe forms of assistance:

- **Feeding back:** give information about performance.
- **Hints:** offer clues while withholding full solution or detailed instructions.
- **Instructing:** tell what to do or how and why to do it.
- **Explaining:** provide elaboration or clarification.
- **Modeling:** demonstrate behavior for possible imitation.
- **Questioning:** invite an active linguistic and cognitive response. A rhetorical question or a button press is not automatically evidence of that response.

These categories overlap in practice; explain a borderline case rather than forcing a unique label. A demonstration can organize reasoning, reduce task demands, or illustrate a planning strategy. The same visual cue can merely report state or direct attention toward a relation, depending on its use. Do not fill every category or invent absent affective needs.

**Classification tension:** the 2010 prose first states five intentions and six means, then says “six” intentions while enumerating A–E. Table 1 and the actual enumeration support five intentions; this guide follows those, not a fabricated sixth. The review also synthesizes earlier classifications rather than reproducing Wood et al.'s six original functions unchanged. Its account of those functions is secondary evidence, not direct review of the 1976 article.

## What the classroom evidence changes

The 2010 review covers face-to-face human scaffolding in primary/secondary education, with literature searched in October 2009 for 1998–2009 publications. Most empirical work was descriptive. Limited effectiveness studies, often one-to-one and using comparatively straightforward tasks, were encouraging but did not establish universal effectiveness. Measurement remained a central problem: definitions and operationalizations often differed, and coding tutor actions alone missed interaction.

The 2015 classroom study extends that evidence under more naturalistic conditions; it is not a replacement taxonomy. Thirty teachers and 768 students aged 12–15 took part in a five-lesson Dutch pre-vocational social-studies project. Students worked in small groups on open-ended EU tasks. Teacher training increased contingency and also increased groups' time working without teacher support. Subsequent analyses therefore used measured contingency and independent working time rather than treating assignment to training as a clean single-factor explanation.

Read the conditional results, not just the abstract's broad achievement language:

- Neither achievement measure confirmed the predicted occasion-by-contingency interaction. Without controlling for task effort, the three-way interaction with independent working time was also not significant.
- **Multiple-choice achievement:** a three-way interaction appeared only when task effort was included as a covariate. With short independent working time, lower contingency accompanied greater gains; with long independent working time, higher contingency accompanied greater gains (Figure 3). This was not a universal benefit of either low or high contingency.
- **Knowledge assignment:** the three-way interaction remained non-significant, including with task effort controlled. Do not generalize the multiple-choice pattern to all understanding outcomes.
- **Task effort:** frequent low-contingency support accompanied an increase. With long independent working time, effort declined at both contingency levels, less with high contingency (Figure 4). “Smaller decline” is not an increase.
- **Appreciation of support:** higher contingency related to greater appreciation; this does not establish game enjoyment, emotion, or mastery.

The study focused on contingency, not a full longitudinal demonstration of all three scaffolding characteristics. Authors explicitly call for more than two time points to establish gradual fading and responsibility transfer. Short duration, cognitive subject-matter focus, and linear analyses limit interpretation; no optimum independent-working interval was established.

**Game implication is a question:** how do support fit, interruption frequency, and opportunity to work alone interact in this slice? Do not import classroom time values as hint delays or interpret “low contingency” as an instruction to give mismatched help. Teacher availability, peer interaction, subject matter, and school incentives differ from optional game assistance.

**Theoretical tension:** the 2015 introduction describes ZPD as constructed through collaborative interaction. Chaiklin instead argues that interaction identifies maturing functions relevant to development, rather than creating the zone in that simple sense. Keep this disagreement visible. Use the 2015 study for its operational classroom findings, not to erase the developmental distinction.

## Inputs and interpretation

Roles: `skill-atoms`, `challenges`, `levels`, `experiments`.

Inspection topics: skill atoms, challenge routes, and relevant source records.

Read concept/mode and selected records before interpreting help. Use [model contract](../workflows/model.md) and [review contract](../workflows/review.md); guide application does not authorize record edits, experiment execution, or design adoption.

- **Concept:** intended experience, audience assumptions, discovery/retry goals, and unresolved assistance. Player story is intent or illustration unless backed by actual evidence; do not treat it as a participant report or translate it wholesale into MDA aesthetics.
- **Skill atoms:** intended understanding, expression, and evidence sought. Prerequisites describe authored relationships, not assessed psychological functions. Rank is authored placement, not developmental age or capability.
- **Challenges:** selected `entry`/`next` route, learning-role links, and body assistance context. `introduces`, `requires`, and `tests_mastery` name intentions; none certifies teaching, impossibility, or mastery. Levels are optional grouping, not mandatory lesson stages.
- **Experiments:** separate proposed hypotheses/methods from actual observations/results. An accepted record with no observations is not empirical support. Inspect relevant rule/feedback and `control-vars` owners when interpreting what a hint reveals or costs; do not duplicate configuration in this guide.

Absent assistance records mean unknown assistance, not no assistance in play. No level records need not block route discussion. Malformed links are data issues; valid empty links are not evidence of inability. Declared routes are opportunities, not certified gameplay reachability.

## Apply to a selected support episode

1. **Bound the question.** Name one intended understanding and support episode, plus a short later opportunity. State route, source state, concept goal, and exclusions. Done: “help with switch routing after bypassing practice” is bounded; “find every player's ZPD” is not.
2. **Separate claims before proposing help.** List authored understanding/route facts, observed behavior if any, and unknowns about prior familiarity or assistance. Define independent performance for this task without assigning a mastery state. Done: assumptions cannot masquerade as observed inability.
3. **Describe the suspected obstacle and alternatives.** Is the proposal about noticing state, interpreting a relation, planning, or carrying out an action? Cite evidence or label it hypothetical. Include at least one competing explanation where material: unclear feedback, deliberate exploration, or misunderstood objective can resemble missing understanding. Done: help targets a stated uncertainty rather than a generic “struggling player.”
4. **Specify an intention–means pair.** Explain what support reveals, what it takes over, and what meaningful decision remains. Compare keeping current support, optional practice, or no new help when relevant. Done: a reviewer can tell how the proposal differs from supplying the solution.
5. **Describe the response loop.** State what response would inform the next offer of help, how that interpretation could be checked, and when evidence is too ambiguous. In a proposed playtest, this might be a facilitator asking for a plan and checking its meaning; it need not become a shipped dialogue. Done: adaptation is tied to evidence, not elapsed time or an inferred capability profile.
6. **Trace fading and responsibility separately.** Identify a later attempt with less specific help and who chooses the next step. Preserve optional help and recovery. Identify a changed task for possible transfer, while noting what else changes. Done: “hide the hint after completion” is no longer the whole learning claim.
7. **Return a bounded artifact.** State support hypothesis, provenance, alternatives, disconfirming evidence, and unresolved inputs. Offer investigation only; measures, hypotheses, criteria, and observations belong inside an authorized experiment, not new collections or framework scores. Done: user can review proposal without any source adoption or record write.

[Switch-practice](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/challenges/switch-practice.md) introduces read-switch and route-switch and then links to combined-room. [Combined-room](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/challenges/combined-room.md) requires plan-push, route-switch, and coordinate; its body leaves prior planning support undocumented. [Route-switch](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/skill-atoms/route-switch.md) seeks explanation and repeatable choice, explicitly not observed mastery. No physical level map establishes route legality.

[Concept](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/concept.md) uses `game-explorer` mode and a candidate experience of thoughtful commitment and satisfaction from making a plan work. Assistance presentation is unresolved. Its player story is hypothetical, not a play observation. Both [planning investigation](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/experiments/energy-encourages-planning.md) and [route timing investigation](../examples/crate-puzzle/game-design/experiments/route-cost.md) remain unexecuted; timing assumptions do not simulate human understanding.

**Interpretation:** direct route bypasses one documented practice opportunity. It does not establish missing knowledge, an impossible challenge, or a broken ZPD. Requiring practice would change player choice and needs discussion, not automatic route repair.

**Hypothetical scenario, not game facts:** suppose a future playable combined-room supports an optional hint after a player requests help. A facilitator asks what the player expects crate placement to change. If the player describes exit opening but cannot identify what must keep the switch active, a prompt could direct attention to the switch's current state without naming a push sequence. Actual geometry, feedback, and answer remain unspecified; this scenario assumes they can make that relation inspectable.

Possible review artifact in chat or an authorized report:

> **SUPPORT-01 — possible opportunity; origin: agent; severity: advisory, no demonstrated learning defect.**
>
> **Scope/provenance:** current example challenge links and concept, cited above; route-switch understanding only. No play observations. No source edit proposed as an adopted fact.
>
> **Hypothesis:** an optional relation-focused hint may help some players test how crate placement sustains switch state while preserving route bypass and planning choice. This is task-specific assisted learning, not a developmental ZPD assessment.
>
> **Proposed strategy:** cognitive structuring through a hint. Reveal a relevant state relation, not a move list. Player still selects placement and next action. Verify existing presentation first; no new game mechanic assumed.
>
> **Contingency proposal:** use request plus player explanation to choose whether to clarify state, offer a stronger demonstration, or leave space for an attempt. Request alone does not identify the obstacle. If feedback is unreadable, address that interpretation before prescribing more instruction.
>
> **Fading/responsibility proposal:** if a later response shows the player can identify the relation with less help, try an optional broad reminder to inspect state, then an unaided attempt. Retain access to stronger help if that interpretation fails. Player chooses the plan and whether to request help; observer checks who supplies the key relation. This is a proposed sequence, not evidence that fading occurred.
>
> **Changed-task opportunity:** hypothetically alter spatial arrangement while retaining the same switch relation. Record other changed demands so failure cannot be attributed solely to learning. No such layout is authored here.
>
> **Counterevidence/alternatives:** an unaided explanation and correct plan before help weakens the missing-relation account. Success only by replaying a demonstrated sequence does not support independent application. Optional switch-practice or clearer feedback may address a different problem; forcing practice is not the default.
>
> **Next investigation, not execution:** compare what players do before help, with help, and later without that specific help. Missing build/geometry and support details block a concrete protocol. Any retained method and local measures belong in an experiment with separate observations/results.

## Evidence and counterchecks

Follow the 2010 review's measurement warning: inspect both sides of interaction and more than one timescale. Do not turn this into a reproduced questionnaire or computed scaffolding index.

- **Episode scale:** retain task state, support offered, learner response, interpretation, and next support. A hint count alone cannot show contingency. Include non-verbal actions and relevant feedback, not only dialogue.
- **Across attempts:** compare support amount/specificity on related tasks and learner-initiated planning/checking. Less help on an easier task does not by itself show fading. Leaving a player alone does not establish transferred responsibility.
- **Changed-task scale:** distinguish repeated actions, use of the same relation in a new layout, and unrelated task success. Explain which demand stays constant and which changes. A transfer claim needs more than assisted completion.
- **Intervention fidelity:** check that intended assistance was actually delivered, available, noticed, and used as assumed before interpreting outcomes. An assigned hint condition is not evidence that its support process occurred.
- **Separate outcomes:** task completion, explanation, independent application, effort, and appreciation answer different questions. Neither faster completion nor appreciation proves understanding or a better intended experience.

The 2015 study operationalized contingency in teacher–student–teacher three-turn sequences, then aggregated to teacher/occasion; it did not diagnose each student's latent capability. It measured time without teacher support separately from contingency. Those choices illustrate why unit and aggregation matter; they are not a game telemetry specification. Ambiguous player behavior must remain ambiguous unless an authorized method can resolve it.

## Practical questions and common mistakes

- What relation is the player meant to discover, and which part would help supply? Avoid “hint” as an unexplained box.
- What response would make this help unnecessary or inappropriate? Avoid escalating assistance merely because a move failed.
- Is the proposed support responsive to a checked interpretation, or simply always present? A fixed tutorial can be useful support without satisfying this scaffolding model.
- Does a worked example expose reasoning or merely an action sequence? Avoid confusing technical imitation in Chaiklin's account with rote copying.
- Which decisions move back to the player? Avoid equating fewer prompts with transferred responsibility.
- Does support interrupt exploration, prescribe a plan, or make optional practice compulsory? Check concept goals rather than assuming efficient instruction is the only value.
- Does “independent” mean no specific hint, no facilitator, or no help of any kind? Define the boundary; remembered demonstrations, peer help, and persistent UI cues complicate claims.
- Could a cue be an ongoing accessibility aid or core state information rather than temporary instruction? Do not remove needed access or feedback to manufacture a fading sequence.
- Does the finding concern task learning or qualitative development? Use assisted learning/scaffolding language when no developmental account exists.
- Did a classroom effect depend on a covariate, outcome, or time context? Preserve those conditions rather than announcing “adaptive hints work.”
